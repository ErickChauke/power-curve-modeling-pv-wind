Data Exploration: PV & Wind Power Curve Grids¶

Erick Chauke

This notebook loads the four source power-curve grids (PV1, PV2, Wind1, Wind2) and looks at what is actually in them: shape, range, missingness, and any data-quality issues, before anything else is built on top of them. No fitting, no statistics beyond counts and averages, just the data, shown a few different ways (heatmaps, tables, basic numbers).

Basic Descriptive Statistics¶

No modeling here, just counts and averages: how full each grid is, and the range of power each plant actually produced.

stats_rows = []
for sheet_name, g in grids.items():
    power_grid = g["power_grid"]
    n_missing = int(np.isnan(power_grid).sum())
    n_filled = power_grid.size - n_missing
    stats_rows.append({
        "Plant": sheet_name,
        "Grid cells": power_grid.size,
        "Filled cells": n_filled,
        "Fill rate (%)": round(100 * n_filled / power_grid.size, 1),
        "Min power (MW)": round(np.nanmin(power_grid), 2),
        "Max power (MW)": round(np.nanmax(power_grid), 2),
        "Mean power (MW)": round(np.nanmean(power_grid), 2),
    })
stats_df = pd.DataFrame(stats_rows)
print(stats_df.to_string(index=False))

What this shows¶

The filled cells form a diagonal band running from (low irradiance, low temperature) to (high irradiance, high temperature). The missing corners, a cold module with high irradiance, or a hot module with almost no irradiance, are combinations that essentially never occur in the field, since irradiance is what heats the module in the first place. This is a sampling artifact of physically coupled inputs, not a data-quality problem to fix, there is simply no measurement to look up for those input combinations.
