Wind Power Lookup: Wind1 & Wind2¶

Erick Chauke

Same approach as 02_pv_power_lookup.ipynb, applied to the two wind plants: a heatmap, a 3D view, the lookup table itself, and a nearest-measurement lookup function, no fitting anywhere. The one difference from PV: wind direction is circular (15 deg and 360 deg are neighboring bearings, not far apart), so the lookup treats the direction axis that way when finding the closest measurement.

Looking Up a Value¶

The direction axis wraps around (360 deg is next to 15 deg, not far from it), so the lookup measures direction distance the short way around the compass, not by plain subtraction.

In [6]:
sample_queries_wind1 = [(10, 180), (7.3, 95), (18, 358)]
for velocity_query, direction_query in sample_queries_wind1:
    result = nearest_lookup(table_wind1, "wind_direction", "wind_velocity",
                             x_query=direction_query, y_query=velocity_query, circular_x=True)
    print(f"Query: velocity={velocity_query} m/s, direction={direction_query} deg  ->  "
          f"closest measurement: velocity={result['wind_velocity']:.1f}, "
          f"direction={result['wind_direction']:.0f}, power={result['power']:.2f} MW")

Why the Circular Handling Matters¶

A direction query of 358 degrees is genuinely close to both 360 and 15 on the compass. Measuring distance by plain subtraction would treat 358 as far from 15 (a difference of 343) and miss that they are actually neighbors, potentially matching the wrong measurement. The circular distance used above picks the shorter way around the circle instead, so a query near the 360/15 wrap-around point still finds its true nearest neighbor rather than one on the opposite side of the grid.
